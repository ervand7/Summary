Cache invalidation means removing or marking cached data as stale when the source data changes.

### Why is it difficult?
- Race conditions: Concurrent reads and writes can reintroduce stale data.
- Distributed caches: Multiple replicas or cache layers must stay consistent.
- Failures: Cache updates or invalidations can fail.
- Missed invalidation: Developers may forget to invalidate affected keys.

### Common Strategies
1. TTL: Automatically expire entries after a period. Simple, but allows stale data.
2. Delete on write: Invalidate cached entries after DB updates. Common with cache-aside.
3. Write-through: Update the DB and cache on every write. Requires handling partial failures.
4. Versioned keys: Change the cache key when data changes, making old entries obsolete.

### Interview Takeaway
Cache invalidation is difficult because keeping cached copies synchronized with the source of truth
introduces race conditions, consistency challenges, and failure scenarios in distributed systems.
