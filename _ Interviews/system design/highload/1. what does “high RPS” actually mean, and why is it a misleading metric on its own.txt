RPS measures how many requests a system processes per second. High RPS doesn't necessarily mean high load.

Key factors:

1. Complexity: 10k RPS for `/health` ≠ 10k RPS for `/checkout`.
2. Latency: Same RPS with p99 = 20ms vs 2s means very different performance.
3. Concurrency: Higher latency means more in-flight requests.
4. Error rate: 10k RPS with 0.01% vs 5% errors.
5. Resources: CPU, memory, DB connections, external dependencies.

Formula: `Concurrency ≈ RPS × Average Latency (seconds)`

Interview answer:

> RPS alone doesn't indicate system load. I evaluate it together with request complexity, latency (p95/p99),
concurrency, error rates, and resource utilization.
